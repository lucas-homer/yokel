# State rulemaking research inventory

Research inventory compiled 2026-10-14 from the retained audit, integration spike, benchmark rounds 2–19, decisions, architecture, and shared contracts. This is a synthesis of bounded inspections and purposive samples, not a coverage certification. “Not tested” means the cited work does not establish it.

## 2. Pipeline-stage findings

### Discovery and acquisition

- Structured source data should be actively sought. CT exposes working JSON routes wired by its own page; its open list lacks period GUIDs, while history includes separate GUIDs/windows. Treat the route as observed website backend, not a supported contract. CT’s initial rendered extraction missed rows; changed render-wait and full-content settings recovered them, but the experiment did not isolate which change mattered ([audit](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md); [spike CT notes](../state-rulemaking-spike/ct/NOTES.md)).
- Collection surfaces have different jobs: CA HTML contents aids enumeration; NV’s four-week index aids change discovery but requires cumulative recovery; UT RSS is a bulletin trigger; WA’s issue index is useful but row/category counts need normalization. A successful HTTP 200 can still be a homepage, empty/incomplete table, or not-found page ([audit README](../state-rulemaking-audit/README.md), retrieval discussion).
- Artifact lineage, original bytes, hashes, requested/final URL, content type, retrieval time, and failed attempts should be retained. The spike’s 37-artifact evidence set supports deterministic replay; transformed Markdown is not an original-byte archive ([spike README](../state-rulemaking-spike/README.md), [`artifacts.json`](https://github.com/lucas-homer/yokel/blob/research/state-rulemaking-evidence-2026-10/docs/research/state-rulemaking-spike/artifacts.json)).
- Full-register discovery recall, agency/process coverage, pagination completeness, feed delivery, paid-source operational access, physical-mail scanning, and longitudinal update detection remain unmeasured. A successful fetch/parser run is not proof of source completeness ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md); [benchmark protocol](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/PROTOCOL.md)).

### Segmentation and parsing

- Native/structured extraction first is supported for validated formats: CT JSON mapping and guarded WA HTML parsing produced deterministic candidates. Firecrawl aided rendering/readability and Mississippi OCR, but its WA output added no essential value in the tested templates. Mississippi Firecrawl Markdown did not reliably preserve checkbox selection; visual inspection found amendment/hearing/stamp evidence missing or ambiguous in text ([spike README](../state-rulemaking-spike/README.md)).
- Early five-case prompt-only extraction had role errors: WA withdrawal filing date became effective date; MS hearing-request period became comment deadline; TN accommodation period was used as comment evidence and an effective date was invented. MA invented an ID and returned wrong JSON type; AK returned stringified nulls. This was a selected diagnostic, not an error rate ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md#extraction-experiment-and-limitations)).
- PDF benchmark: 20 selected units/16 original PDFs/34 submitted pages, 8 states. On 19 reference-eligible notices, strict passes were native 14, Firecrawl 10, Llama Agentic 18, Agentic Plus 16. Round 2 reported Mistral OCR 18/19 diagnostic and 7/7 fresh eligible; Llama Cost-effective 17/19 and 7/7 fresh; native 14/19 and 7/7 fresh; Docling 15/19 and 5/7 fresh. Purposive, correlated samples and agent-reviewed references forbid national generalization; the wider 3,042-page originals were not all parsed ([benchmark README](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/README.md); [round 2](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round2/README.md)).
- Native text is a cheap first path, not universal truth: it can be empty for scans or omit marks/stamps. Docling is complementary; it recovered some evidence but introduced losses. Firecrawl and LlamaParse tiers also made errors. Preserve source bytes and visual review for decisive form marks; never equate readable output with correct interpretation ([round 2](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round2/README.md); [NOTICE-MATRIX](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/NOTICE-MATRIX.md)).
- Whole-register segmentation and document-boundary recall were not benchmarked. Inputs were complete short notices or bounded excerpts chosen and frozen before parser runs; this avoids claiming a parser result is a collector/segmenter result ([benchmark PROTOCOL](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/PROTOCOL.md)).

### Field/date extraction and normalization

- Keep date roles separate: filing, publication, hearing, written comments, hearing-request, accommodation, operational-policy, effective, and relative periods are distinct. Audit errors and R4–R7 assembly findings show lexical matching and exact quotes alone do not associate a date with the correct role ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md); [round 4](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round4/README.md); [round 6](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round6/README.md)).
- Preserve source wording, date-only versus local datetime versus instant versus relative precision, timezone wording, condition/anchor, and unresolved status. CT epoch decoding is reproducible but does not settle legal clock meaning. WA written-comment date remains date-only; hearing clocks may have unresolved zone. Do not manufacture end-of-day UTC or calculate an unapproved relative period ([spike SPEC](../state-rulemaking-spike/SPEC.md); [CT notes](../state-rulemaking-spike/ct/NOTES.md); [WA notes](../state-rulemaking-spike/wa/NOTES.md)).
- MS’s “20 days after filing” hearing-request condition and “25-day” written-comment period are separate. The anchor/counting rule for the latter remained unresolved. MA’s “EST” wording for an October date may conflict with local daylight time; retain wording and flag, do not silently reinterpret. TN has separate hearing, pre-hearing written-submission, and accommodation periods ([spike README](../state-rulemaking-spike/README.md); [audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md)).
- Four worked normalizations and 41 typed observations (37 deterministic, 4 manual visual) validate an envelope and replay pipeline, not a production normalization policy. The validator checks structure/evidence integrity, not semantic entailment ([normalization examples](https://github.com/lucas-homer/yokel/blob/research/state-rulemaking-evidence-2026-10/docs/research/state-rulemaking-audit/normalization-examples.json); [spike validation report](https://github.com/lucas-homer/yokel/blob/research/state-rulemaking-evidence-2026-10/docs/research/state-rulemaking-spike/validation-report.json)).
- Round 6 produced zero normalized records from four notices: one request-size rejection and three inventory assembly failures. Failures included weekday-prefixed dates, non-contiguous endpoint components, and alternatives constrained to submission routes. R7 made these representable with exact source components, weekday/date consistency, and purpose-scoped routes; it did not show improved real-model extraction ([round 6](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round6/README.md); [round 7](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round7/README.md)).

### Condition and scope extraction (rounds 10–19)

- R10 source-first inventory plus record matching found two previously missed losses in a reused WI notice (repeal/recreation and Zoom/phone relationships), but completeness, source scope, contact roles, and production fidelity remained unverified. R11 specified source-only scope, role, relationship, uncertainty, and reverse-audit rules; its executable tests validate supplied judgments, not semantic discovery ([R10](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round10-source-first/README.md); [R11](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round11-scope-refinement/README.md)).
- R12’s one fresh WA source pilot failed: both source-only and direct arms exhausted 8,192 output tokens and produced invalid/truncated JSON; downstream stages were skipped. R13’s compact source inventory completed (91 units, 5,728 tokens) on the exposed same notice and reconstructed evidence exactly, but semantic review still found identity/uncertainty gaps. It was an engineering feasibility result, not blind quality evidence ([R12](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round12-automated-scope/README.md); [R13](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round13-compact-inventory/README.md)).
- R14 established an offline semantic contract but stopped before dispatch because credential cause was unresolved. R15 recommended full anchored opaque clauses as preservation baseline and typed logic as optional; initial schema was incompatible with the unchanged checker. R16 produced compatible offline wire format `r16.w2.1`, round-tripping 32 valid and 5 invalid cases, but did not initially verify live provider compatibility ([R14](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round14-semantic-contract/README.md); [R15](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round15-condition-preservation/README.md); [R16](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round16-wire-compat/README.md)).
- R17–R19 then tested clause-only extraction on three new WA Register notices, but full typed logic was untestable (only 4 compound conditions versus the predeclared 5 needed). R17 failed mechanically on two same-role conditions; R18 fixed output shape but lost exception text; R19 retained exception text only too broadly and encoded critical “only” restriction as `if` rather than `onlyif`. Adjudicators agreed exactly on scored relations, but the bar failed and S3 was never run. The paid clause-only track stops; any restart needs fresh preselected sources ([clause-only R16–R19](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/clause-only-r16-r19/README.md)).

### Review, abstention, cost and operations

- Unknowns should be typed and visible, invalid shapes rejected, source conflicts retained, and unsupported/ambiguous claims routed to review. The spike uses `candidate`, `needs_review`, `reviewed_for_research` (not production approval); exact quote/structure checks are necessary but insufficient. R3 verifier pilot had zero automatic acceptance from its deterministic baseline; Jev flagged more injected/observed errors than Nano but both accepted a same-parser-corrupted email, and Jev over-escalated a correct control ([spike SPEC](../state-rulemaking-spike/SPEC.md); [R3](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round3/README.md)).
- R4’s conservative routing accepted zero fields and did not call Jev; R5 shadow-accepted six rows/five claims, of which independent source review found only two fully correct. R6’s verifier was never called because extraction/assembly failed. R8 Wisconsin extraction truncated at output cap and stopped without repair; R9 exact-ID response structure worked but missed known completeness gaps. These are distinct failure stages, not one normalized accuracy metric ([R4](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round4/README.md); [R5](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round5/README.md); [R6](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round6/README.md); [R8](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round8/README.md); [R9](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round9-live-exact-id/README.md)).
- Costs are job/sample specific: round 2 recorded 168 Llama Cost-effective credits (about $0.21 documented list-price equivalent) and $0.11585 OpenRouter reported usage for 28 notices; this is not invoice verification or production cost per usable notice. R10 reported $0.115117 usage and $0.323840 conservative ledger debit for two calls. R12 reported $0.182347 for two truncated attempts; R13 $0.066281 for one compact call. R17–19 reported $0.2517365 total provider usage, $1.08 conservative ledger debit; invoices were not checked. Do not compare across changing interfaces/versions as causal tiers ([round 2](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round2/README.md); [R10](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round10-source-first/README.md); [R12](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round12-automated-scope/README.md); [R13](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round13-compact-inventory/README.md); [R16–19](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/clause-only-r16-r19/README.md)).
- Operational controls matured: frozen inputs/references, hashes, exact output retention, durable job IDs, bounded concurrency, stop-on-failure, no blind retry, offline reconciliation and signed research-job releases. These show controlled experiment execution, not unattended state collection or supported provider service. R2’s historical Firecrawl audit retained a 151/152 stop-rule caveat; R2 REST follow-up passed offline operations checks ([API-RUNNER](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/API-RUNNER.md); [benchmark README](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/README.md); [ADR 0012](../../decisions/0012-stable-research-job-release.md)).

## 3. Established vs. not measured

### Established within the inspected samples

- At least one useful public JSON path exists in CT; several state sources expose HTML indexes, PDF issues/notices, feeds, tables, or vendor-hosted free access. Access, format, scope, cadence, completeness, and licensing are separate dimensions.
- Same proceeding may have multiple windows; index labels are not event types; one row need not be one unique notice; withdrawn/partial events must preserve target and scope. WA and CT examples demonstrate these directly.
- Parsing can recover decisive evidence from native text, OCR, and visual review, with different failure modes. No tested parser is universally reliable.
- Date-role/condition errors, schema/type errors, output truncation, incomplete source inventory, and parser corruption are independently observable failure modes. Provenance, hashes, exact evidence and review states make replay and correction possible.
- The research artifacts provide reusable evidence-backed fixtures and offline code, while explicitly keeping state ingestion research-only and production eligibility/action readiness false.

### Explicitly not established

- Full-register or statewide discovery recall/precision; all-agency/process scope; annual or weekly notice denominator; state-by-state source volume; stable feed/API contracts; page/row completeness; missed/duplicate notice rates.
- Longitudinal update/change detection, reconciliation of amendments/extensions/reopenings over time, or source outage recovery in production.
- Held-out legal date normalization accuracy; relative-day/business-day calculations; jurisdiction time-zone policy; legal sufficiency of submission channels, anchors, cutoff inclusivity, or daylight-time wording.
- Representative national parser/extractor accuracy, human-gold quality, independent labeling reliability, legal clause interpretation, or quality of complete proposed rule text.
- Automated routing’s safe acceptance rate, review workload reduction, reviewer time, production throughput, or total operating cost per usable notice/state. Vendor cost claims were not invoice-verified; some cohorts/settings differ.
- Automated access/redistribution rights or sustained access for NJ, AR, paid/vendor-hosted, email, newspaper, and physical-document routes.
- A transferable statewide normalization contract or production adapter; no state research was merged into federal contracts or production ingestion.

## 5. Fit with DocketClock architecture

### What transfers conceptually

- Append-only evidence observations, immutable payload hashes, versioned derived windows, explicit provenance, conflict/unknown states, and review as a new observation align with the state spike’s evidence envelope and source findings. Existing model is designed to preserve raw source payload, parser version and exact dates ([architecture lines 23–29, 50–67](../../architecture/docketclock.md); [contracts lines 423–453, 489–513, 517–587](../../../packages/contracts/src/index.ts)).
- Many-to-many observation-to-window targeting already supports one notice affecting multiple participation windows (contract lines 426–428, 506–513). The state evidence strengthens need for multiple windows per proceeding and typed lifecycle relations, not a mutable single deadline.
- Confidence is first-class and not model-scored; unknown/conflicting states, source review verdicts, and constraints against fake close dates are present ([contract lines 233–258, 338–413, 543–637](../../../packages/contracts/src/index.ts)). The spike demonstrates exact `text_exact` and `visual_review` evidence modes, method/version and review state ([spike schema lines 73–139](https://github.com/lucas-homer/yokel/blob/research/state-rulemaking-evidence-2026-10/docs/research/state-rulemaking-spike/observation.schema.json)).
- OCD IDs are intended as stable cross-system keys, with source-specific identifiers retained. This provides an extension seam, but state IDs require a namespaced versioned grammar and reviewed merge/split policy; they cannot use today’s federal-only parser unchanged.

### Concrete gaps and hard constraints

- **Hard federal-only decision:** ADR 0003 says state/local expansion requires a funded jurisdiction tranche; architecture describes the rule as a literal no-state adapter boundary ([ADR 0003 lines 15–29](../../decisions/0003-docketclock-substrate-first.md); [architecture lines 12–16, 27–32](../../architecture/docketclock.md)). This inventory does not change that decision.
- **Source enum:** `ObservationSource` currently allows only `federal_register`, `regulations_gov`, `govinfo`, `human_review` (contract lines 282–292). State source registry/source IDs and publisher/source lineages need an additive contract design.
- **OCD-ID:** validator and `makeOcdId` hard-code `ocd-participation-window/federal/{frDocNum|regs:...}` (lines 294–328). It cannot mint `US-XX` state windows or source-native aliases today. The research spike’s jurisdiction regex `US-XX` is a separate research-only field, not shared support (spike schema lines 14–17).
- **Window/assertion model:** current `ParticipationWindow` is deadline-centric and includes FR/Regs identifiers and raw date slots (`raw_fr_close_date`, `raw_regs_close_datetime`), with a federal comment/hearing taxonomy (lines 551–587). State examples need date-only/instant/local/relative temporal unions, anchors/counting policies, source locations, condition/scope, event/notice/proceeding identities, and multiple participation channels.
- **Date assumptions:** current schema’s operative close is UTC datetime (nullable) and architecture pins conflict comparison to America/New_York (architecture lines 39, 59–63; contract lines 564–570). State text includes date-only deadlines, differing zones, relative durations, cutoffs and submission conditions; do not force them into the federal UTC close before a reviewed policy exists.
- **Human review:** the federal review verdict can pin a close date or resolve specified federal conflict/lifecycle cases, with reviewed payload hashes (contract lines 337–413). State review must be able to adjudicate event type, role, scope/conditions, identity, date precision/time zone and visual marks—not only pin a close.
- **Decision/release governance:** ADR 0012 authorizes only reviewed research jobs within owner-authorized scope/budgets and maintains job immutability/credential boundaries. It is not authorization to run state acquisition or alter production contracts ([ADR 0012](../../decisions/0012-stable-research-job-release.md)).

## 6. Open questions and contradictions

- **Scope of “state coverage”:** rulemaking notices versus hearings, petitions, guidance, operational policy, permits, adjudications, legislation and agency-only programs are separate universes. The audit recommends defining an explicit per-agency/process/time/source promise before choosing adapters ([audit README](../state-rulemaking-audit/README.md)).
- **Audit count drift is explained by time, not a contradiction:** CT open list was five in the September 30 audit and four in the October 5 spike; PR2024-059 disappeared from the open list while history retained two periods. Open-list absence is not closure/withdrawal evidence ([audit README](../state-rulemaking-audit/README.md); [spike README](../state-rulemaking-spike/README.md)).
- **“No public API” language is too broad:** documented API absence can coexist with CT’s observed website JSON endpoint and AL’s `/api/` route returning a publication PDF. These are not equivalent to supported normalized notice APIs ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md)).
- **CA “no index” versus HTML contents:** the July summary shorthand understated monthly HTML contents that the detailed research had documented. Treat HTML as useful enumeration metadata, not full detail or completeness proof ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md)).
- **NJ “free PDF/paywalled/vendor-locked”:** OAL archive is not proven statewide source; official Register access is described as free despite vendor hosting; separate statewide database exists but target retrieval failed. Access price, automation, rights and completeness remain distinct unresolved axes ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md)).
- **State difficulty tiers and national counts:** inherited “cheap/hard” labels, scrapeable percentages, weekly national volumes, and whole-state print/paywall claims are unsupported by the fresh sample and should not be carried into roadmap estimates ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md)).
- **Parser score versus readiness:** Agentic/Mistral’s strongest sample scores do not establish national accuracy, discovery recall, update monitoring, correct legal role, complete rule text or safe action. Round 2 separately found an email typo outside the frozen benchmark gate; exact destinations need explicit evaluation ([round 2](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round2/README.md)).
- **R16–19 final status:** R16 initially said provider compatibility unverified; R17–19 later verified strict-mode HTTP 200 for four calls but failed the clause-only semantic bar. The later evidence closes only provider compatibility, not extraction quality. The whole track stops pending fresh preselected sources ([R16](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/round16-wire-compat/README.md); [clause-only final](../state-rulemaking-benchmark/clause-only-r16-r19/README.md)).
- **Date role and calculation policy:** source wording may name “EST” in a daylight-time month; relative comment and hearing-request periods may have different anchors/counting rules. Research retains these as warnings/unknowns; no jurisdiction-wide legal interpretation was validated.
- **Contract transfer:** audit says architecture concepts transfer but existing source enum, federal ID grammar, FR/Regs fields and Eastern assumptions do not. The spike is intentionally separate; no research observation was added to shared contracts ([audit findings](../state-rulemaking-audit/audit-findings.md); [spike README](../state-rulemaking-spike/README.md)).
