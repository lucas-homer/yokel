5. Short Title: Nursery and Nursery Stock

6. (a) Summary of what the rule says and of any proposed amendments including whether the rule implements a state statute for the first time:

The proposed rule readopts and substantively amends the Agr 2500 Nursery and Nursery Stock rules. The revision modernizes and reorganizes the regulatory framework governing the licensing, inspection, certification, quarantine, and enforcement requirements for nurseries, plant dealers, landscapers, and other entities handling nursery stock. Major amendments include expanded and restructured definitions aligned with RSA terminology; a redesigned licensing framework establishing an electronic application and renewal system; new application timelines and late-fee provisions; detailed inspection authorities including references to prohibited invasive species lists; a fully rewritten quarantine section with expanded operational requirements; updated procedures for interstate and international certification via the federal phytosanitary certificate issuance and tracking(PCIT) system; and expanded enforcement provisions addressing violations such as false information, obstruction, and failure to pay fees. Minor amendments include organizational changes, clarifying edits, updated statutory citations, and alignment of terminology.

6. (b) Brief description of the groups affected:

The proposed rules affect nurseries, plant dealers, landscapers, and other businesses that grow, store, sell, distribute, or ship nursery stock within or from New Hampshire. Entities engaged in interstate or international shipment of nursery stock will be affected by updated certification requirements. Businesses located within or handling regulated articles from quarantine areas will be subject to expanded quarantine compliance obligations. Independently owned businesses with annual gross sales of rooted plant material will be affected by revised licensing fees and new electronic licensing procedures, including deadlines and late fee requirements. State agencies and universities exchanging nursery stock, as well as not for profit arboreta, remain exempt from licensing fees.

3. Cost and benefits of the proposed rule(s):

The proposed rules increase the annual plant dealer licensing fee from $25 to $50 for licensees with annual gross sales greater than $3,000 and establish a $25 annual licensing fee for licensees with annual gross sales of $3,000 or less, who are currently exempt from the licensing fee. Based on 792 licensees with annual gross sales greater than $3,000, the $25 fee increase may result in an additional $19,800 in annual revenue
