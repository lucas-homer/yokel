                         FISH AND BOAT COMMISSION
                                    [58 PA. CODE CH. 51]

               Administrative Provisions; Fiscal Code Note Provisions

                                         [56 Pa.B. 1571]
                                   [Saturday, March 21, 2026]

  The Fish and Boat Commission (commission) proposes to amend Chapter 51 (relating to
administrative provisions). The commission is publishing this proposed rulemaking under the
authority of 30 Pa.C.S. (relating to Fish and Boat Code). This rulemaking proposes to add
Subchapter O to memorialize the fund flexibility created under section 1736-E of The Fiscal Code
(72 P.S. § 1736-E).

A. Effective Date

  This proposed rulemaking, if approved on final-form rulemaking, will go into effect upon
publication in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

B. Contact Person

  For further information on this proposed rulemaking, contact Robert J. Schefter, Esq., P.O. Box
67000, Harrisburg, PA 17106-7000, (717) 705-7810. This proposed rulemaking is available on the
commission's website at https://www.pa.gov/en/agencies/fishandboat.html.

C. Statutory Authority

  This proposed amendment to Chapter 51 is published under the statutory authority of 30 Pa.C.S.
§§ 521 and 531 (relating to establishment and use of Fish Fund; and establishment and use of Boat
Fund). The specific authority for the funding flexibility memorialized in the proposed amendment is
published under the statutory authority of section 1736-E(a) and (b) of The Fiscal Code.

D. Purpose and Background

   The Fiscal Code (72 P.S. §§ 1—1810.10005) was amended in 2025 to permit the commission to
use Fish Fund money for expenditures related to boats and boating that also benefit fish and fishing
and Boat Fund money for expenditures related to fish and fishing that also benefit boats and boating
in section 1736-E(a) and (b) of The Fiscal Code. Previously, no crossover of funds was permitted in
30 Pa.C.S. § 521 or § 531, leading to inefficiency and higher costs for boaters and anglers.

   Section 1736-E(a) and (b) of The Fiscal Code is a permanent provision of The Fiscal Code and
grants the commission much needed flexibility in funding its mission. However, The Fiscal Code is
a voluminous legislative document not regularly referenced by commission staff. The commission
needs to memorialize this funding flexibility in its own regulations so that staff members have
proper notice of these provisions of The Fiscal Code. This proposed rulemaking satisfies the need by
memorializing in the commission's regulations the funding flexibility granted by The Fiscal Code
and providing notice thereof in a manner that could not be achieved by less formal means. This
                                                                                                       1/3
                                                 PA Bulletin, Doc. No. 26-402

proposed rulemaking does not grant any new authority to the commission; rather it simply
memorializes the authority granted by The Fiscal Code.

E. Summary of Proposal

   The commission has the authority to create an administrative provision to permit the crossover of
funds to ensure efficient processes and reasonable costs for boaters and anglers, which is not
otherwise provided for in the Fish and Boat Code. The commission proposes that Chapter 51 be
amended to include Subchapter O to memorialize the fund flexibility created by The Fiscal Code as
set forth in Annex A. A new subchapter is warranted because there are no other subchapters in
Chapter 51 related to funding, and Subchapter O and § 51.151 (relating to Fish Fund) are the next
chronological notations. The language used in the proposed rulemaking in § 51.151 is adopted
directly from section 1736-E(a) of the The Fiscal Code, and the language used in § 51.152 (relating
to Boat Fund) is adopted directly from section 1736-E(b) of The Fiscal Code.

F. Paperwork

  This proposed rulemaking will not increase paperwork and will not create new paperwork
requirements.

G. Fiscal Impact

  This proposed rulemaking will have no adverse fiscal impact on the Commonwealth or its
political subdivisions.

H. Public Comments

  Interested persons are invited to submit written comments, objections or suggestions about this
proposed rulemaking to the Executive Director, Fish and Boat Commission, P.O. Box 67000,
Harrisburg, PA 17106-7000, within 30 days after publication of this notice in the Pennsylvania
Bulletin. Comments submitted by facsimile will not be accepted.

  Comments also may be submitted electronically to ra-pfbcregulations@pa.gov. Electronic
comments submitted in any other manner will not be accepted.

      § 51.151. Fish Fund.
         Notwithstanding 30 Pa.C.S. § 521 (relating to establishment and use of Fish Fund), under section

      1736-E(b) of The Fiscal Code (72 P.S. § 1736-E(b)), money in the Boat Fund may be expended by
      the commission for activities related to fish and fishing that benefit boats and boating.

A. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code § 51.7 (Relating to Public Comment at Commission Meetings).

Commentary:
     The Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) proposes to amend 58 Pa.
     Code § 51.7 (relating to public comments at Commission meetings) to direct that public
     comments for virtual meetings will be accepted on a date prior to the meeting via
     telephonic message or other appropriate format determined by the presiding officer.
     This amendment also ensures that the meeting agenda is published prior to the public
     comment period.

       Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code § 51.7 be amended as follows:

       § 51.7. Relating to public comments at Commission meetings.

       (a) Public comments at meetings of the full Board.

          (1) General rules. The Commission will provide a brief public comment period at the
              beginning of each public meeting. The Commission ordinarily will accept public
              comments following its committee reports and prior to its consideration of the formal
              agenda. The Commission may limit comments to matters that are within the
              Commission's jurisdiction that are not repetitive or duplicative of other comments. The
              Commission president or presiding officer will request members of the public wishing to
              comment to indicate prior to the start of the meeting that they wish to provide public
              comment. The Commission president or presiding officer will determine the appropriate
              time limit for individual comments and announce that limit prior to receiving any
              comments. If members of the public prepare a written statement, they will be asked to
              provide a copy to the designated staff person. Members of the public representing a
              specific organization will be requested to designate one spokesperson to present their
              collective comments.

          (2) Consideration of agenda items. The Commission ordinarily will not permit public
              comments during the course of its meetings when it considers agenda items or other
              matters before the Commission. The Commission president or other presiding officer
              may invite brief public comments on items other than regulatory actions when he
              determines that allowing the comments will be useful in the best interests of the
              Commission.

                                             *****

       (b) Public comments at committee meetings. The committee chair will provide a brief
           public comment period at the beginning of each committee meeting. The committee
           chair will determine an appropriate format for this period, including its length. The
                                                                                             7


           committee chair may limit comments to matters that are related to the charge of
           the committee that are not repetitive or duplicative of other comments.

           (1) Public comments at virtual committee meetings. A virtual meeting is one
               conducted using digital technology that allows participants to communicate in
               real time from remote physical locations. For virtual committee meetings, the
               committee chair will provide a public comment period to be held prior to the
               date of the meeting. The format for public comments will be by telephonic
               voice message or other appropriate format determined by the committee
               chair. The meeting agenda and the public comment period and format shall be
               provided with the public notice of the meeting.

Briefer:
        Robert Schefter, Chief, Office of Chief Counsel

Recommendation:
     Staff recommend the publication of a notice of proposed rulemaking containing the
     amendment as described in the Commentary. If adopted on final rulemaking, this
     amendment will go into effect upon publication of a second notice in the Pennsylvania
     Bulletin.

B. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code § 51.6 (Relating to Petitions or Requests for Regulations).

Commentary:
     The Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) proposes to amend Title 58
     Pa. Code § 51.6 (relating to petitions or requests for regulations) to provide further
     instruction on returned proposals and to direct that the initial staff review will be the
     final administrative adjudication of the Commission for proposals that do not meet the
     threshold substantive requirements set forth in § 51.6 (b)(1).

       Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code § 51.6 be amended as follows:

       § 51.6. Relating to petitions and requests for regulations.

       (a) Form. Petitions or requests for regulations addressed to the Commission shall be
            submitted in the format set forth in 1 Pa. Code § 35.18 (relating to petitions for
            issuance, amendment, waiver or deletion of regulations).
       (b) Initial staff review.
            (1) The staff will review every petition for regulations to ensure that:
                 (i) It is complete as required by 1 Pa. Code § 35.18.
                 (ii) The petition requests an action within the authority or jurisdiction of the
                      Commission.
                (iii) The petition does not conflict with applicable Commonwealth or Federal law.
            (2) If the staff determines the petition is not acceptable for one of the reasons set
                 forth in paragraph (1), it will return the petition to the petitioner together with a
                 statement of the reason why it is not acceptable.
                 (i) If the petition is being returned as incomplete pursuant to paragraph
                       (b)(1)(i) of this section, the statement shall indicate that the petition may
                       be resubmitted upon completion.
                 (ii) If the petition is being returned as noncompliant with paragraph (b)(1)(ii) or
                       (iii) of this section, the statement shall indicate that the Commission will
                       take no further action on the petition.
            (3) A staff determination of noncompliance with paragraph (b)(1)(ii) or (iii) of this
                 section will be considered the final administrative adjudication of the
                 Commission on the petition. This section supersedes 1 Pa. Code § 35.20.

       (f) Commission action. The staff shall submit the petition, the staff report, the
       petitioner’s response, if any, the staff response, if any and, in the case of boating
       regulations, the advice and opinions of the Boating Advisory Board to the Commission
       for consideration as part of the agenda of the next regular meeting occurring more than

          30 days after the file is deemed complete. The Commission will determine what action
          to undertake with respect to the petition at a public meeting. The action taken by the
          Commission will be considered the final administrative adjudication of the
          Commission on the petition.

C. Disposition of Property at Ingham Springs, Solebury Township, Bucks County.

Commentary:
     The Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, by and through the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat
     Commission (Commission), owns 0.245 acres known as Ingham Springs (Springs) in
     Solebury Township (Township), Bucks County. The Springs (also known as Aquetong)
     are located 1.25 miles east of New Hope as depicted on Exhibit F (Location Map).

          The Commission previously owned 47 acres adjacent to the Springs. In 2009, the
          Commission sold most of the acreage to the Township, excluding the Springs, which the
          Commission retained, and the Township leased as depicted on Exhibit G (Site Map). The
          Commission retains a conservation easement on the 47-acre site to protect the aquatic
          resource as depicted on Exhibit H (Conservation Easement Map).

          Since 2009, the Township has performed remediation and stream improvements in the
          area. The site is currently used as a park and natural area. The Township has
          successfully pursued multiple sources of funding for restoration and park improvements
          and has conducted invasive species removal, along with various other improvements to
          the buildings and roads located on the property.

          To date, the Commission has retained control of the Springs to protect the aquatic
          resource and had previously considered using property as a water source for a hatchery.
          However, the Commission has determined that it will not require the Springs for a
          hatchery. The Township recently obtained a fair market value appraisal of $3,700 for the
          Springs. If the sale of the Springs to the Township is approved, the Commission will
          retain a conservation easement on the Springs, as well as a right of first refusal should
          the Township decide to sell the Springs in the future.

          The Township will be responsible for all costs associated with the conveyance including
          recording costs. Staff will adhere to the Commission’s standard practices for the
          disposition of the property.

Briefer:
        Penny Ayers, Chief, Division of Public Access and Property Services

Recommendation:
     Staff recommend that the Commission authorize the disposition of the property at
     Ingham Springs, Solebury Township, Bucks County, as described in the Commentary.

B. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code § 93.13 (Issuing Agents).

Commentary:
     The Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) may designate issuing agents
     for the issuance of temporary boat registrations to the public. Pursuant to 58 Pa. Code
     § 93.13, applicants interested in becoming an issuing agent for temporary boat
     registration must submit an application, a one-time fee and a bond in favor of the
     Commission.

       Fishing license agents, as authorized by 58 Pa. Code § 51.35, may submit a bond “or
       other security in a form satisfactory to the Commission,” typically a letter of credit from
       a bank, which may be more efficient and less expensive to secure than a bond. 58 Pa.
       Code § 93.13 requires a bond only and does not permit other forms of security. This
       proposed amendment will bring §93.13 into alignment with § 51.35 and provide
       flexibility for a more efficient onboarding process for temporary boat registration issuing
       agents.

       Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code § 93.13 be amended as follows:

       § 93.13. Issuing agents.
       (a) Designation.
                                            *****

          (2) An applicant seeking to become an issuing agent for temporary boat
              registrations shall complete and submit to the Commission an application on the
              form prescribed by the Commission and shall pay a one-time, nonrefundable fee
              of $250. Applications shall be accompanied by a bond in favor of the
              Commission, or other security in a form satisfactory to the Commission, in a
              minimum amount specified by the Executive Director or a designee.

                                            *****

       On March 23, 2026, the Boating Advisory Board considered this proposal and
       recommended the Commission approve the publication of a notice of proposed
       rulemaking containing the amendment.

A. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code Chapter 63 (General Fishing Regulations).

Commentary:
     Per Addendum III to Amendment 7 of the Fishery Management Plan for Atlantic Striped
     Bass, the Atlantic States Marine Fisheries Commission requires that states define total
     length for the purposes of measuring Atlantic Striped Bass to determine compliance with
     size limits associated with both commercial and recreational fisheries. Standardization is
     needed, as different measurement methods can yield different results, potentially
     leading to enforcement complications.

          The Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) advises anglers how to
          measure fish in the Pennsylvania Fishing Summary book; however, no formal definition
          is promulgated in Title 58. Rather than define total length measurement specifically
          under 58 Pa. Code § 61.2 (Delaware River, West Branch Delaware River and River
          Estuary) for a single species (i.e., Striped Bass), staff recommend a definition be applied
          to the measurement of all fish across the Commonwealth.

          Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code Chapter 63 be amended by adding § 63.58 as follows:

          § 63.58. Measuring fish.

          When measuring fish to determine compliance with established size limits, the total
          length shall be determined as defined herein.

          Total length is defined as the greatest straight-line length as measured on a fish while
          laid flat on its side with its mouth closed from the most forward tip of the jaw or snout
          to the farthest extent of the tail with the upper and lower tail lobes compressed or
          squeezed together.

Briefer:
        Kristopher Kuhn, Director, Bureau of Fisheries

     Staff recommend the publication of a notice of proposed rulemaking containing the
     amendment as described in the Commentary. If adopted on final rulemaking, this
     amendment will go into effect upon publication of a second notice in the Pennsylvania
     Bulletin.

B. Amendments to 58 Pa. Code § 61.1 (Commonwealth Inland Waters), § 65.19 (Stocked Trout
   Waters Open to Year-Round Fishing), § 65.24 (Miscellaneous Special Regulations) and §
   65.26 (Extended Trout Season).

Commentary:
     Under 58 Pa. Code § 61.1 (Commonwealth inland waters), the minimum size limit (MSL)
     for trout is seven inches. The MSL for trout was last amended in 1983 as part of the
     Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission’s (Commission) Operation Future when the MSL
     was increased from six inches to the current seven-inch MSL to provide additional
     protection to wild Brook Trout populations.

      Staff evaluated wild Brook Trout data collected through electrofishing surveys over the
      past ten years and determined an increase in MSL for trout from seven inches to nine
      inches would provide additional protection to wild Brook Trout. Based on this analysis,
      an increase in MSL to nine inches for trout would prohibit harvest of more than 99% of
      the state’s wild Brook Trout.

      Staff also evaluated angler opinion data that was collected through the Commission’s
      2023 Pennsylvania Trout Angler Survey (Trout Survey), and these data suggest most
      trout anglers support an increase in the MSL for trout. As part of the Trout Survey,
      anglers where specifically asked, “Would you support or oppose an increase of the
      minimum to nine inches to provide protection of wild Brook Trout?” and 85% of all trout
      anglers responded that they would support an increase in the MSL to protect wild Brook
      Trout. Harvesting trout, regardless of species or origin (i.e., stocked versus wild), was
      not ranked as one of the top three reasons trout anglers fish in Pennsylvania.

      Given the support from Pennsylvania’s trout anglers and the increased protection that
      will be afforded to wild Brook Trout, staff recommend the MSL for trout be increased to
      nine inches. Amending size limits for trout requires minor amendments to various
      sections referencing trout size limits. As such, staff recommend reformatting the size
      limits in regulations relating to trout size limits for clarification and simplification.

      Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code § 61.1 be amended as follows:

      § 61.1. Commonwealth inland waters.

       (d) Except as otherwise provided in this subpart, the following seasons, sizes and daily
      limits apply to inland waters of this Commonwealth and the Youghiogheny Reservoir:

C. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code § 65.24 (Miscellaneous Special Regulations): Add Ohio River
   Basin.

Commentary:
     The Three Rivers (Ohio, Allegheny, Monongahela rivers) system in Western Pennsylvania
     supports diverse fishing opportunities for a variety of species in and around the
     metropolitan area of Pittsburgh. Once severely polluted, the Three Rivers now boast
     excellent populations of Walleye, Sauger, Smallmouth Bass, Muskellunge, Flathead
     Catfish and Channel Catfish that provide countless hours of recreational fishing.

      In 2022, the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) initiated a Blue
      Catfish reintroduction program on the Three Rivers. Blue Catfish, which are native to
      the Three Rivers but did not return following their extirpation in the early 1900s, are
      currently being stocked on the Ohio River, with plans to expand the program to the
      Allegheny and Monongahela rivers in 2027. The goal of the reintroduction is to
      establish a self-sustaining, naturally-reproducing population of Blue Catfish in the Three
      Rivers.

      Recent assessments of the Flathead Catfish populations in the Three Rivers conducted
      to estimate abundance, age and growth rates, and mortality characterize the
      populations as low-to-moderate density that exhibit extreme longevity. Flathead
      Catfish in the Three Rivers system are the oldest ever recorded in Pennsylvania, with
      individuals over 30 years of age relatively common. In addition to their longevity,
      Flathead Catfish growth in the Three Rivers is extremely slow. On average, it takes a
      Flathead Catfish 15 years to reach 24 inches in length, and over 30 years to reach 35
      inches, which is considered a trophy fish desired by anglers.

      Current regulations in the Three Rivers system for Blue Catfish and Flathead Catfish,
      among other species of catfish, include them as panfish, with a 50-fish-per-day limit and
      no minimum size limit. As the goal of Blue Catfish reintroduction is to establish a
      naturally reproducing population, maximizing survival of Blue Catfish stocked in the
      Three Rivers is paramount, and staff recommend implementing catch-and-release
      regulations on Blue Catfish in all flowing waters in the Ohio River Basin. Also, to prevent
      potential overexploitation of Flathead Catfish and conserve trophy-sized fish, staff
      recommend a four fish daily creel limit for Flathead Catfish, with only one fish being
      greater than or equal to 35 inches in length for all flowing waters in the Ohio River
      Basin.

D. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code § 69.24 (Limitations on Gear).

Commentary:
     Pennsylvania’s portion of Lake Erie supports a thriving Yellow Perch commercial
     fishery, and there are three commercial trap net licenses issued by the Pennsylvania
     Fish and Boat Commission (Commission). The commercial fishery is regulated by the
     Commission in Subchapter C (Commercial fishing licenses) and Subchapter D
     (Commercial fishing, seasons and nets) in 58 Pa. Code Chapter 69 (Fishing in Lake
     Erie and boundary lakes).

       In 2024, the Commission amended 58 Pa. Code Chapter 69 following a systematic
       review by staff which identified amendments that streamlined regulatory
       compliance while continuing to provide resource protection to the Lake Erie fishery.
       However, in 2025, staff consulted the three licensed commercial fishers and
       determined an additional amendment is warranted that would allow three license
       holders to operate from the same boat.

       Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code § 69.24 be amended as follows:

                                                *****
       § 69.24. Limitations on gear.

        (a) A licensed trap netter may not use or fish more than six trap nets, singularly or in a
       series or tandem set, at any one time.

        (b) No more than [two] three licensed trap netters may fish from the same boat at any
       one time. If two or more licensed trap netters fish from the same boat at the same time,
       the trap netters shall clearly and distinctly separate their catch into containers or boxes
       marked with their respective license numbers.

A. Addition of Hammond Lake, Tioga County, to the Panfish Enhancement Program (58 Pa.
Code § 65.11) for Crappies.

Commentary:
     Hammond Lake, a 640-acre impoundment owned by the United States Army Corps of
     Engineers and managed by the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission), is
     located in Tioga and Middlebury Townships, Tioga County, just southeast of the town of
     Tioga. The dam impounds Crooked Creek at river mile 2.78 upstream from its
     confluence with the Tioga River.

          Panfish evaluations conducted in 2024 documented robust Black Crappie and White
          Crappie populations, with the majority of the fish captured being greater than 10 inches
          in total length. Given unique size structure and the propensity of anglers to harvest
          crappies, a more restrictive regulation is needed to conserve and enhance the fishery.
          As such, it is recommended this reservoir be managed with Panfish Enhancement
          angling regulations (58 Pa. Code § 65.11) for crappies. Panfish Enhancement regulations
          allow for year-round harvest of crappies (Black Crappie and White Crappie) greater than
          or equal to nine inches in length with a daily limit of 20. When Panfish Enhancement
          regulations in a waterway apply to one species, the creel limit is 20 for that species with
          the total creel not to exceed 50 panfish combined. All other fish species not managed
          under these special regulations will be managed under Commonwealth Inland Waters
          angling regulations (58 Pa. Code § 61.1).

          A notice of proposed designation was published at 56 Pa.B. 1526 (March 14, 2026)
          (Exhibit I). The Commission received one public comment in support of the proposal. A
          copy of the public comment was provided to the commissioners.

Briefer:
        David Nihart, Chief, Division of Fisheries Management

Recommendation:
     Staff recommend that the Commission add Hammond Lake, Tioga County, to the Panfish
     Enhancement program (58 Pa. Code § 65.11) for crappies as described in the
     Commentary. If approved, the designation will go into effect upon publication of a
     second notice in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

                                             NOTICES
                            FISH AND BOAT COMMISSION
             Proposed Changes to List of Class A Wild Trout Streams; April 2026

                                               [56 Pa.B. 975]
                                        [Saturday, February 14, 2026]

   The Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) is considering changes to its list of Class A wild trout
streams. Under 58 Pa. Code § 57.8a (relating to Class A wild trout streams), it is the Commission's policy to
manage self-sustaining Class A wild trout populations as a renewable natural resource to conserve that
resource and the angling it provides. Class A wild trout populations represent the best of this Commonwealth's
naturally reproducing trout fisheries. With rare exceptions, the Commission manages these stream sections
solely for the perpetuation of the wild trout fishery with no stocking.

  (1) Criteria developed for Class A Wild Trout fisheries are species specific.

  (2) Wild Trout Biomass Class Criteria include provisions for:

  (i) Wild brook trout fisheries.

  (A) Total brook trout biomass of at least 30 kg/ha (26.7 lbs/acre).

   (B) Total biomass of brook trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha (0.089
lbs/acre).

  (C) Brook trout biomass must comprise at least 75% of the total trout biomass.

  (ii) Wild brown trout fisheries.

  (A) Total brown trout biomass of at least 40 kg/ha (35.6 lbs/acre).

   (B) Total biomass of brown trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha (0.089
lbs/acre).

  (C) Brown trout biomass must comprise at least 75% of the total trout biomass.

  (iii) Mixed wild brook and brown trout fisheries.

  (A) Combined brook and brown trout biomass of at least 40 kg/ha (35.6 lbs/acre).

   (B) Total biomass of brook trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha (0.089
lbs/acre).

   (C) Total biomass of brown trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha (0.089
lbs/acre).

  (D) Brook trout biomass must comprise less than 75% of the total trout biomass.

  (E) Brown trout biomass must comprise less than 75% of the total trout biomass.

   (iv) Wild rainbow trout fisheries. Total biomass of rainbow trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length
of at least 2.0 kg/ha (1.78 lbs/acre).

  (v) Mixed wild brook and rainbow trout fisheries.

  (A) Combined brook and rainbow trout biomass of at least 40 kg/ha (35.6 lbs/acre).

   (B) Total biomass of brook trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha (0.089
lbs/acre).

   (C) Total biomass of rainbow trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha (0.089
lbs/acre).

  (D) Brook trout biomass shall comprise less than 75% of the total trout biomass.

  (E) Rainbow trout biomass shall comprise less than 75% of the total trout biomass.

C. Proposed Changes to the List of Wild Trout Streams (58 Pa. Code § 57.11).

Commentary:
     During recent surveys, Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) staff
     documented wild trout populations in 39 new waters and in six waters that are currently
     listed, qualifying them for listing or limit revision as wild trout streams under 58 Pa.
     Code § 57.11. Exhibit L provides information on the locations and a data summary for
     each of these waters.

          A notice of proposed changes was published at 56 Pa.B. 978 (February 14, 2026) (Exhibit
          M). The Commission received a total of two public comments regarding the proposed
          additions and revisions: all support the proposal. Copies of public comments were
          provided to the commissioners.

Briefer:
        David Nihart, Chief, Division of Fisheries Management

Recommendation:
     Staff recommend that the Commission add 39 streams or portions of streams to its list
     of wild trout streams and revise the section limits of six waters that are currently listed
     as set forth in the notice of proposed changes. If approved, these additions and
     revisions will go into effect upon publication of a second notice in the Pennsylvania
     Bulletin.

          Proposed Changes to the List of Wild Trout Streams; April 2026

                                  [Saturday, February 14, 2026]

  Under 58 Pa. Code § 57.11 (relating to listing of wild trout streams), it is the policy of the Fish
and Boat Commission (Commission) to accurately identify and classify stream sections supporting
naturally reproducing populations of trout as wild trout streams. The Commission's Fisheries
Management Division maintains the list of wild trout streams. The Executive Director, with the
approval of the Commission, will from time-to-time publish the list of wild trout streams in the
Pennsylvania Bulletin. The listing of a stream section as a wild trout stream is a biological
designation that does not determine how it is managed. The Commission relies upon many factors in
determining the appropriate management of streams.

   At the next Commission meeting on April 27, 2026, the Commission will consider changes to its
list of wild trout streams. Specifically, the Commission will consider the addition of the following
streams or portions of streams to the list:
