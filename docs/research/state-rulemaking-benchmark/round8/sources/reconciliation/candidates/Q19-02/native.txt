Executive Director’s Report
Executive Director Timothy Schaeffer provided his quarterly report to the commissioners. The
27th Class of Waterways Conservation Officer Cadets completed their curriculum at Mansfield
University in December and will graduate in July; thanks were given to Commissioner Anderson
for attending the ceremony.

Director Schaeffer thanked the General Assembly, especially Senator Rothman, for sponsoring
Senate Bill 800, which provides flexibility between the Fish Fund and Boat Fund. The legislation
allows funds to be used for cross purposes when there is a clear benefit to both anglers and
boaters with full transparency and public reporting required in the Commission’s annual report.
The goal of the fund flexibility is to improve efficiency and reduce the need for fee increases for
anglers and boaters.

The Executive Director also mentioned that the Commission is submitting its annual report to
the legislature as required, with a House Game and Fisheries Committee hearing scheduled for
February 4 at 9:00 a.m. The annual report will include QR codes linking to videos for added
engagement, such as the one previewed during these remarks. Director Schaeffer concluded
his report with a thank you to Commission staff and partners for working together.

Notice of Temporary Changes to Fishing Regulations
Chief Counsel Robert Schefter provided notice to the Commissioners that the Executive
Director used his authority under 58 Pa. Code § 65.25 to enact temporary fishing regulation

questions, and the report was concluded.

A. Amendment to 58 Pa. Code Chapter 51 (Administrative Provisions).

Commentary:
     The Pennsylvania Fiscal Code (Act of April 9, 1929 (P.L. 343, No. 176), as amended)
     (Fiscal Code) was amended in 2025 to permit the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat
     Commission (Commission) to use Fish Fund moneys for expenditures related to boats
     and boating that also benefit fish and fishing and Boat Fund moneys for expenditures
     related to fish and fishing that also benefit boats and boating. (Fiscal Code § 1736-E (a)
     and (b)). Previously no crossover of funds was permitted, leading to inefficiency and
     higher costs for anglers and boaters.

         Staff propose that 58 Pa. Code Chapter 51 be amended to memorialize the fund
         flexibility created by the amendment to the Fiscal Code by adding Subchapter O as
         follows:

         Subchapter O. FISCAL CODE PROVISIONS

         Sec.
         51.139. Fish Fund.
         51.140. Boat Fund.

         § 51.139. Fish Fund.
             Notwithstanding 30 Pa.C.S. § 521 (relating to establishment and use of Fish Fund),
         pursuant to § 1736-E(a) of the Pennsylvania Fiscal Code (Act of April 9, 1929 (P.L. 343,
         No. 176), as amended), moneys in the Fish Fund may be expended by the Commission
         for activities related to boats and boating that benefit fish and fishing.

         § 51.140. Boat Fund.
             Notwithstanding 30 Pa.C.S. § 531 (relating to establishment and use of Boat Fund),
         pursuant to § 1736-E(b) of the Pennsylvania Fiscal Code (Act of April 9, 1929 (P.L. 343,
         No. 176), as amended), moneys in the Boat Fund may be expended by the Commission
         for activities related to fish and fishing that benefit boats and boating.

Briefer:
        Robert Schefter, Chief Counsel, Office of Chief Counsel

A. Removal of Blue Hole Creek, Section 02, Somerset County, from the Stocked Trout Waters
   Open to Year-round Fishing program (58 Pa. Code § 65.19).

Commentary:
     Blue Hole Creek, Somerset County, is a three-mile-long stream that originates near the
     Fayette and Somerset county border in the Forbes State Forest and flows southeast to
     its confluence with Falls Creek near the Borough of Rockwood, Somerset County. The
     stream is currently managed as two sections for fisheries management purposes.
     Section 01 is managed under Commonwealth Inland Waters angling regulations (58 Pa.
     Code § 61.1). Section 02 is included in the Pennsylvania Fish and Boat Commission’s
     (Commission) Stocked Trout Waters (STW) program and managed with Stocked Trout
     Waters Open to Year-round Fishing (STW-OYR) angling regulations (58 Pa. Code §
     65.19).

         In 2025, the Commission was notified by the Pennsylvania Department of
         Environmental Protection (DEP) of results from its physicochemical and
         macroinvertebrate community survey that documented high concentrations of
         cadmium and lead in a portion of Blue Hole Creek. While these results do not
         necessitate the DEP to issue a consumption advisory because it does not have a
         standard for such advisories, staff will remove Section 02 from the STW program for
         2026 due to potential health safety concerns. As such, staff recommend Blue Hole
         Creek, Section 02, be removed from the STW-OYR program given that it will no longer
         be stocked with trout by the Commission.

         A notice of proposed designation was published at 55 Pa.B. 8447 (December 13,
         2025) (Exhibit F). The Commission received no public comments relating to this
         proposal.

Briefer:
        David Nihart, Chief, Division of Fisheries Management

Recommendation:
     Staff recommend that the Commission remove Blue Hole Creek, Section 02, Somerset
     County, from the Stocked Trout Waters Open to Year-round Fishing program (58 Pa.
     Code § 65.19) as described in the Commentary. If approved, the section will revert to
     Commonwealth Inland Waters angling regulations (58 Pa. Code § 61.1) upon publication
     of a second notice in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

Other New Business
Commissioner Anderson requested that fisheries management staff evaluate the Stony Creek
River for potential changes to its trout management strategy. The request follows multiple
letters and inquiries from constituents in his district. There is interest in adding a new stocked
trout water section in northern Somerset County near Greenhouse Park. He asked that this
area be scheduled for a fisheries survey in the coming year so staff can assess it and respond to
anglers in the Somerset and Cambria County area.

Commissioner Kauffman commended Mark Sweppenhiser, Tigh Savercool and staff for their
work on implementing the newly passed abandoned and derelict boat policy. He highlighted a
recent virtual meeting with the Philadelphia Parking Authority and praised staff for effectively
addressing questions and concerns during what he described as a challenging but productive
meeting. Thanks were given to State Representative Solomon and Karen O’Connor of the
Philadelphia Parking Authority for their leadership and collaboration. The meeting included
broad participation from the Philadelphia caucus which helped vet the program, clarify details
and move implementation forward. Executive Director Timothy Schaeffer added that following
the new legislation, tools, forms and processes are now in place to assist municipalities
statewide in addressing abandoned and derelict vessels. He said the agency plans to continue
to reach out to communities across Pennsylvania and thanked the General Assembly and
Governor for supporting the legislation.

                             PROPOSED RULEMAKING
                                  FISH AND BOAT COMMISSION
                                            [ 58 PA. CODE CH. 53 ]

                             Commission Property; Access Areas and Marinas

                                                 [55 Pa.B. 8375]
                                          [Saturday, December 13, 2025]

            The Fish and Boat Commission (commission) proposes to amend Chapter 53 (relating to
         commission property). The commission is publishing this proposed rulemaking under the authority
         of 30 Pa.C.S. (relating to Fish and Boat Code) (code). The proposed amendment would cancel the
         commission's restrictions that apply to the North East Access and Marina, which is no longer owned
         and managed by the commission.

         A. Effective Date

           This proposed rulemaking, if approved on final-form rulemaking, will go into effect upon
         publication in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

         B. Contact Person

           For further information on this proposed rulemaking, contact Robert J. Schefter, Esq., P.O. Box
         67000, Harrisburg, PA 17106-7000, (717) 705-7810. This proposed rulemaking is available on the
         commission's website at https://www.pa.gov/en/agencies/fishandboat.html.

         C. Statutory Authority

            The proposed amendment to § 53.12a (relating to access areas and marinas) is published under
         the statutory authority of section 741 of the code (relating to control of property).

         D. Purpose and Background

           The specific purpose and background of the amendment are described in more detail in the
         summary of proposal.

         E. Summary of Proposal

            The commission has the authority to create rules and regulations that provide for the health and
         safety of those who boat and fish on or near the waters of this Commonwealth. The Lake Erie
         tributaries are some of the most popular boating and fishing areas in this Commonwealth. Currently,
         under § 53.12a, it is unlawful to hunt on the commission's property in Northeast Township, Erie
         County, which covers approximately 17.68 acres and is known as the North East Access area and
         Marina (North East Marina Property).

            On June 18, 2025, through a notational vote, the commission voted to authorize the disposition of
         the North East Marina Property to the North East Community Marina Authority (NECMA) and
                                                                                                                1/3
12/15/25, 1:20 PM                                          PA Bulletin, Doc. No. 25-1672

         fishing access. On July 9, 2025, the commission disposed of the North East Marina Property to
         NECMA and acquired an easement from NECMA. As a result, the commission is no longer
          responsible for the control, management and operation of the property.

             The commission proposes that § 53.12a be amended to read as set forth in Annex A.

          F. Paperwork

            This proposed rulemaking will not increase paperwork and will not create new paperwork
          requirements.

          G. Fiscal Impact

            This proposed rulemaking will have no adverse fiscal impact on the Commonwealth or its
          political subdivisions.

                                                   NOTICES
                                FISH AND BOAT COMMISSION
                                  Proposed Special Regulation Designation

                                                    [55 Pa.B. 8447]
                                             [Saturday, December 13, 2025]

            The Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) has approved guidelines with regard to
         encouraging public participation on possible changes to the designation of streams, stream sections
         or lakes for special regulation programs. Under 58 Pa. Code Chapter 65 (relating to special fishing
         regulations), the Commission designates or redesignates certain streams, stream sections and lakes
         as being subject to special fishing regulations. These designations and redesignations are effective
         after Commission approval when they are posted at the site and a notice is published in the
         Pennsylvania Bulletin. Under the Commission's guidelines, a notice concerning the proposed
         designation of a stream, stream section or lake under special regulations ordinarily will be published
         in the Pennsylvania Bulletin before the matter is reviewed by the Board of Commissioners.

            At the next Commission meeting on January 26, 2026, the Commission will consider taking the
         following action with respect to a water subject to special fishing regulations under 58 Pa. Code
         Chapter 65, effective upon publication in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

         58 Pa. Code § 65.19. Stocked trout waters open to year-round fishing

            The Commission will consider removing the following water from its list of stocked trout waters
         open to year-round fishing regulated and managed under 58 Pa. Code § 65.19 (relating to stocked
         trout waters open to year-round fishing):

                                                    NOTICES
                                   FISH AND BOAT COMMISSION
                    Proposed Changes to List of Class A Wild Trout Streams; January 2026

                                                       [55 Pa.B. 8024]
                                                [Saturday, November 22, 2025]

             The Fish and Boat Commission (Commission) is considering changes to its list of Class A wild trout
          streams. Under 58 Pa. Code § 57.8a (relating to Class A wild trout streams), it is the Commission's
          policy to manage self-sustaining Class A wild trout populations as a renewable natural resource to
          conserve that resource and the angling it provides. Class A wild trout populations represent the best of
          this Commonwealth's naturally reproducing trout fisheries. With rare exceptions, the Commission
          manages these stream sections solely for the perpetuation of the wild trout fishery with no stocking.

             (1) Criteria developed for Class A Wild Trout fisheries are species specific.

             (2) Wild Trout Biomass Class Criteria include provisions for:

             (i) Wild brook trout fisheries.

             (A) Total brook trout biomass of at least 30 kg/ha (26.7 lbs/acre).

             (B) Total biomass of brook trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha
          (0.089 lbs/acre).

             (C) Brook trout biomass must comprise at least 75% of the total trout biomass.

             (ii) Wild brown trout fisheries.

             (A) Total brown trout biomass of at least 40 kg/ha (35.6 lbs/acre).

             (B) Total biomass of brown trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha
          (0.089 lbs/acre).

             (C) Brown trout biomass must comprise at least 75% of the total trout biomass.

             (iii) Mixed wild brook and brown trout fisheries.

             (A) Combined brook and brown trout biomass of at least 40 kg/ha (35.6 lbs/acre).

             (B) Total biomass of brook trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha
          (0.089 lbs/acre).

             (C) Total biomass of brown trout less than 15 cm (5.9 inches) in total length of at least 0.1 kg/ha
          (0.089 lbs/acre).

             (D) Brook trout biomass must comprise less than 75% of the total trout biomass.

             (E) Brown trout biomass must comprise less than 75% of the total trout biomass.

                    Proposed Changes to the List of Wild Trout Streams; January 2026

                                                  [55 Pa.B. 8026]
                                           [Saturday, November 22, 2025]

           Under 58 Pa. Code § 57.11 (relating to listing of wild trout streams), it is the policy of the Fish
         and Boat Commission (Commission) to accurately identify and classify stream sections supporting
         naturally reproducing populations of trout as wild trout streams. The Commission's Fisheries
         Management Division maintains the list of wild trout streams. The Executive Director, with the
         approval of the Commission, will from time-to-time publish the list of wild trout streams in the
         Pennsylvania Bulletin. The listing of a stream section as a wild trout stream is a biological
         designation that does not determine how it is managed. The Commission relies upon many factors in
         determining the appropriate management of streams.

            At the next Commission meeting on January 26, 2026, the Commission will consider changes to
